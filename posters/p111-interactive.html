Nebulised therapies for smoke inhalation injury: mechanistic rationale, evidence gaps, and the case for an adaptive platform trial

Smoke inhalation injury is the single largest preventable driver of burn mortality — yet despite widespread use of nebulised unfractionated heparin, N-acetylcysteine, and β-agonists in specialist burn centres, the underpinning evidence remains fragmented, underpowered, and inconclusive.

Subsequent pneumonia rate
Pneumonia is the most frequent secondary complication of inhalation injury, occurring in approximately 38% of affected patients — a direct downstream consequence of the second-hit fibrin-cast obstruction and impaired mucociliary clearance.
+20%
Absolute mortality increase
Inhalation injury increases burn mortality by approximately 20 percentage points in absolute terms, making it the single largest preventable contributor to burn death in modern series.
ZERO
Therapies of proven survival benefit
Despite 27 years of investigation, no nebulised therapy for smoke inhalation injury has demonstrated definitive survival benefit in adequately powered multicentre randomised trials. The HEPBURN trial — the closest such attempt — terminated at n=13.

Why conventional RCTs fail here
Four structural barriers · together, fatal · tap any barrier
Barrier 01
Low incidence
~5,000 UK cases/year across ~25 specialist centres.
Each centre sees a small annual volume; recruiting an adequately powered trial requires multicentre coordination across the entire UK burn-care network. Compounding this, only a subset of admitted inhalation-injury patients meet eligibility criteria for any given intervention trial.
Barrier 02
Heterogeneity
Severity, TBSA, timing of injury, co-morbidities.
Inhalation injury severity ranges from mild bronchial irritation to ARDS-grade lung injury; TBSA varies; smoke composition differs by exposure context (housefire, industrial, combustion source). Standardising entry criteria sharply narrows the eligible pool, but loosening them dilutes any treatment effect.
Barrier 03
High acuity
Consent + randomisation needed within hours of arrival.
Both first-hit and second-hit pathology are time-sensitive. The therapeutic window for first-hit-targeting interventions (e.g., NAC) is 0–6 hours; for second-hit-targeting (heparin), it extends to 24–72 hours. Patients present unconscious or intubated; consent procedures designed for elective trials fail. Many sites lack 24/7 research staff cover.
Barrier 04
Confounded co-intervention
Heparin is almost always given alongside NAC, salbutamol, and bronchoscopic lavage.
Specialist centres deploy bundled nebulised therapy as standard practice. Single-agent trials therefore study the agent against background polypharmacy, not against no intervention; isolating the contribution of any single agent is methodologically very difficult.
Empirical proof
HEPBURN — the only completed international RCT of nebulised heparin for inhalation injury — was powered for 116 patients across multiple specialist burn centres in two countries. It terminated at n=13.
The adaptive platform solution
A single, perpetual trial — answering many questions at once

A master protocol with multiple intervention domains. Patients are consented once into the platform; Bayesian adaptive randomisation directs them across domains and updates allocations as evidence accrues. Futile arms are dropped; promising arms gain more patients; new agents can be added without restarting the trial.

REMAP-CAP as proof of concept: the adaptive platform design enrolled 13,000+ patients globally during COVID-19, generating multiple practice-changing answers in parallel. The UK burn-care network — concentrated, well-coordinated, and clinically connected — is uniquely placed to apply the same approach to inhalation injury.

A UK-led adaptive platform trial
Embedded across the BBA-affiliated specialist burn-centre network

Platform design

Master protocol; three initial domains (heparin, NAC, β-agonist); Bayesian adaptive randomisation; pre-specified futility / efficacy stopping rules.

Primary outcome

28-day ventilator-free days (compatible with HEPBURN and Hakim 2025); pneumonia, mortality, ICU length-of-stay as secondary.

Feasibility advantage

One consent, one infrastructure, one statistical core. Smaller per-domain samples; perpetual recruitment overcomes the HEPBURN failure mode.

09From mechanism to method.

Traditional RCTs have not resolved — and arguably cannot resolve — uncertainty in the management of smoke inhalation injury. Bayesian adaptive platform trials offer a pragmatic, ethically robust pathway to definitive, practice-changing evidence.

For the clinician

Heparin, NAC, and β-agonists remain reasonable adjuncts in specialist centres on mechanistic grounds — but practice should be acknowledged as evidence-light. Local protocols should support future platform-trial participation.

For the BBA community

The UK burn-care network — concentrated, well-coordinated, and clinically connected — is uniquely placed to lead an international adaptive platform trial. This is the design that can finally close a 27-year evidence gap.
