Poster P117 · Narrative synthesis

Lidocaine infusions in adult burn patients: analgesic efficacy, opioid sparing, and unresolved long-term outcomes

BoardP117
Session3A–3E
DateFri 19 Jun 2026
Time13:09–13:12
LocationLevel 1 Foyer
↓ Download poster (PDF) § Jump to references

01Background

Pain after burn injury is severe, prolonged and frequently under-controlled by opioid-based regimens alone, with consequences extending well beyond the acute admission — opioid-related adverse effects, tolerance, chronic pain, and impaired functional recovery.

Intravenous lidocaine infusions have established analgesic and opioid-sparing effects in perioperative and non-burn contexts. Their use in burn care is expanding, but the evidence base supporting that uptake remains small, short, and inconsistently positive.

02Purpose

Synthesise the current clinical evidence for intravenous lidocaine infusions in adult burn patients, and identify gaps relating to pain control, opioid use, safety, and patient-centred long-term outcomes.

03Methods

Narrative synthesis of randomised controlled trials, observational studies, and systematic reviews / meta-analyses evaluating intravenous lidocaine infusions in adult burn patients. Outcomes assessed: pain intensity, opioid consumption, adverse effects, quality of life, functional recovery.

04Results

The evidence base comprises three RCTs, two systematic reviews / meta-analyses, foundational case series, and a critical narrative review.

  • Wasiak 2011 (n=45, procedural pain): pain scores significantly reduced; opioid demand unchanged.
  • Abdelrahman 2020 (n=19, background pain, 7-day infusion): VAS unchanged; opioid consumption reduced by ~25%.
  • Haghighi 2023 (n=66, acute burn pain): pain scores and opioid consumption both significantly reduced.
  • Wasiak 2014 Cochrane review: insufficient evidence to determine effectiveness.
  • Elgadi 2025 meta-analysis (4 RCTs, n=152): no significant pooled effect on pain or opioid use.
The evidence gap

Sample sizes were small to moderate. Follow-up ranged from procedural timepoints to a maximum of 7 days. No study reported quality-of-life or functional recovery outcomes, and no data were available beyond the acute inpatient phase. No significant safety concerns were identified, although studies were underpowered to detect rare adverse effects.

05Conclusion

Intravenous lidocaine infusions may provide short-term analgesic and opioid-sparing benefits in selected adult burn patients. However, the absence of long-term and patient-centred outcome data represents a major limitation. These findings highlight the need for well-designed prospective trials incorporating extended follow-up and functional recovery outcomes.
